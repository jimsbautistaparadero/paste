 Potholes, 311 Reports, and a Theory of Heterogeneous Resident Demand for City Services (2024)

Related Study:

Synthesis of the Related Studies

The reviewed studies reveal several research areas that are not adequately addressed by conventional emergency and complaintreporting applications.

First, Goh et al. (2019) and Zhou et al. (2026) demonstrate the importance of integrating geospatial information, citizengenerated observations, and decisionsupport capabilities into disaster management systems. Their findings support the development of a platform that can collect incident information, identify affected locations, and provide officials with useful information for emergency coordination.

Second, Kenny et al. (2020) and the lastmile community research (2024) highlight the importance of system accessibility and communication reliability during emergencies. These studies suggest that a reporting application should not depend entirely on continuous internet connectivity and that emergency information must remain accessible to communities with limited technological infrastructure.

Third, Cook et al. (2024), Lee et al. (2021), and Pribadi (2021) demonstrate that digital reporting systems face challenges involving unequal participation, technology adoption, and accessibility. These findings suggest that an effective barangay platform must accommodate different types of residents and should not treat the absence of digital complaints as evidence that no community problems exist.

Finally, Wang et al. (2025) and Saliterer et al. (2026) emphasize the importance of government responsiveness, transparent complaint processing, and accountability. Their findings suggest that digital reporting should extend beyond the initial submission of concerns by providing residents with meaningful information about the status and resolution of their reports.

Research Gap and Contribution of the Proposed Study

An important gap identified across the reviewed literature is the separation between emergency response, ordinary community problem reporting, and public feedback management. Existing studies demonstrate that these areas can benefit from digital platforms, but their findings also reveal limitations concerning offline accessibility, reporting inclusivity, administrative responsiveness, and transparent resolution tracking.

The proposed Design and Development of an Integrated WebBased and AndroidBased Barangay Emergency, Community Problem Reporting, and Feedback Management System intends to address these limitations through a unified platform with the following capabilities:

 Research Dimension             Proposed System Contribution                                                                                           
  
 Emergency Response           Emergency submissions, GPS coordinates, incident photographs, responder coordination, and emergency status monitoring. 
 Community Reporting          Reporting infrastructure damage, sanitation problems, environmental concerns, and other barangayrelated issues.       
 Feedback Management          Resident suggestions, service evaluations, feedback categorization, and administrative responses.                      
 Offline Accessibility          Local report storage and synchronization when internet connectivity becomes available.                                 
 Geospatial Intelligence        Mapping reported incidents, identifying recurring problem locations, and visualizing community concerns.               
 Administrative Accountability  Report assignment, status tracking, action history, resolution documentation, and response monitoring.                 
 Decision Support               Statistical reports, incident trends, unresolved complaints, and community problem summaries.                          

The central contribution of the proposed study is not simply the creation of another reporting application. It is the integration of emergency communication, routine community problem resolution, and public feedback into one coordinated barangay information management platform.

This integration provides a stronger basis for studying how digital technologies can improve not only the submission of community concerns but also their documentation, prioritization, resolution, and evaluation.
